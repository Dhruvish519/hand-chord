    How it works

    1. YCrCb conversionBrightness (Y) is separated from colour (Cr, Cb), so lighting matters less.
    2. Skin thresholdcv.inRange keeps pixels whose Cr and Cb fall in the skin range.
    3. MorphologyMedian blur, then opening removes specks and closing fills holes.
    4. Largest contourcv.findContours; the biggest blob in the box is the hand.
    5. MomentsCentroid gives position; central moments give the tilt angle.
    6. Trained CNNA square crop around the hand (112×112) goes through a MobileNetV3 network with cv.dnn. It outputs 0–5 fingers or "none" (faces, objects, other gestures).
    7. Tilt from the CNNThe same network also outputs the hand’s rotation (as sin and cos), learned by rotating training images by known angles. Tilt drives vibrato.
    8. SmoothingProbabilities are averaged over frames and must pass 55% confidence plus a 7-frame vote before the sound changes.

    The classical method (convexity defects: deep, narrow valleys in the convex hull are gaps between fingers) is still available in the panel for comparison.

    The model

    98.0%test accuracy, upright hands
    95.6%average accuracy at any rotation (was 60.5%)
    5.7°average tilt error
    44,053hand crops · 7,973 people

    Dataset: HaGRID (HAnd Gesture Recognition Image Dataset), 31,833-image sample at 384p (cj-mills/hagrid-sample-30k-384p, CC-BY-SA 4.0). Real photos from thousands of people in their own homes, lighting and backgrounds.

    Labels: fist → 0, one → 1, peace / two-up → 2, three → 3, four → 4, palm / stop → 5. Other gestures, relaxed hands and random background patches → none.

    Split: 80/20 by person: nobody in the 7,933 test crops appears in the 36,165 training crops, so the score reflects new users.

    Training: MobileNetV3-Small, ImageNet-pretrained. Every training image is rotated by a random angle (up to a full turn), slightly perspective-warped, zoomed, flipped and colour-shifted. Because the rotation is known, the network also learns to output the hand’s tilt. Exported to ONNX and run in the browser by OpenCV.js.

    Two outputs, one network: 7 class scores (0–5 fingers, none) plus sin and cos of the tilt angle. Loss = cross-entropy + squared error on (sin, cos).
